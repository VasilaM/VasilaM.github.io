Art & The Cost of Happiness

I learn yet again life’s bitter lesson—every happiness must be paid for with its weight in pain. I may damn my own miserable life, thinking it so exceptionally grand in its woes, as is every mind’s instinct and inclination, but the truth is, even if I were anyone else, the books would still have to be balanced. Such is the nature of truth: there’s no getting away from it.

The wretchedness of our condition almost makes a mind wish for a life so mundane that the score is always settled without incredible exuberance or extraordinary suffering. But this would be a naive grasp at solace. It would mean foregoing the seemingly simple comforts that are most inalienable to us, for it is those comforts that are the source of our pitiful lives’ greatest joy. The warm security of being worried over by our loved ones, the delightful company of our marvelous friends, the little strokes of luck that make everyday life so much more bearable. All these things come at the price of equivalent horror through simple discomforts. A slew of bodily aches, pains, plagues and maladies that follow us at every turn, interpersonal misunderstandings that inevitably sour for some period even our most precious relationships, small inconveniences that make everyday life so much more cumbersome.

But truth does not serve any higher god, pleasure or retribution. It just is, containing multitudes. And so this truth happens to have a silver lining. Pain is a fertile soil for art to flourish in. Of course, the ability to engage in art does not disappear if we are happy. But we care much less to tend to the garden when we are content. The art we make in times of satisfaction is oft shallow and unmoving. Profound reflection, clarity, and compassion come from profound suffering. Without friction we are not challenged to face and meditate on the nature of reality. I cannot muse about whether this art, this revelation, is enough to justify paying for every happiness with equal pain. A world in which we never suffer at the cost of joy but have no art fueled by torment or enlightenment won through agony may very well be better than the one we live in. Regardless, the fact is these are the cards we have been dealt. Every alternate reality we might imagine is equally, infinitely out of reach. If it is solace we seek, we must take it in the value of meaning created through struggle.
